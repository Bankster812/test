# Wholesaling legal triage — TX · FL · GA · OH · NC (2025–2026)

> **NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Informational triage compiled from public sources to
> help you prepare questions for a **licensed attorney** in each state. Statutes
> change and secondary sources sometimes misstate effective dates; confirm every
> load-bearing point against the primary statute and local counsel before
> acting. Items flagged **[VERIFY]** are where sources conflicted or were thin.
> Produced by the platform's `LegalAnalyst` research sub-agent.

## TEXAS
- **License:** No license to assign your own contract **if** you act as a
  principal buyer of an *equitable interest* and don't broker "for another."
  Marketing the *property* (vs. your contract) can require a license. (Occ. Code
  Ch. 1101 / TRELA.)
- **Assignment disclosure (key 2024 change):** **Property Code §5.0205**
  (redesignated from §5.086 by SB 1577, 88th Leg.), effective **Jan 1, 2024**
  **[VERIFY date]**. Requires **written** disclosure to (1) any buyer — that you
  sell only an option/assign a contract and **hold no legal title** — and (2) the
  owner — that you intend to assign.
- **Pre-foreclosure:** **Bus. & Com. Code Ch. 21** (foreclosure consultants):
  homeowner may rescind anytime; consultant may not acquire an interest or take
  power of attorney. Executory-contract rules (Prop. Code §§5.061–5.085) may also
  apply to rent-back/repurchase structures. **[VERIFY which applies.]**
- Sources: statutes.capitol.texas.gov (PR Ch.5, BC Ch.21); trerc.tamu.edu; trec.texas.gov

## FLORIDA
- **License:** No license as principal buyer (§475.011 exemption), **but**
  unlicensed brokerage is a **3rd-degree felony (§475.42)** — Florida enforces.
- **Marketing:** advertise only your equitable interest, never the property.
  **[VERIFY exact marketing-limit section.]**
- **Pre-foreclosure (significant):** **§501.1377** ("equity purchaser") — written
  agreement in **≥12-pt UPPERCASE** before any transfer, copy within 3 hours,
  cooling-off cancellation, 30-day/3-time cure where a repurchase right exists;
  penalties up to **$15,000/violation**.
- **Mini-TCPA:** Florida Telephone Solicitation Act (**§501.059**) is stricter
  than federal — real litigation risk for FL call/text campaigns.
- Sources: flsenate.gov Ch.475; leg.state.fl.us §475.42, §501.1377

## GEORGIA
- **License:** Currently **no** wholesaler-licensing statute (Title 43 Ch. 40 =
  brokerage for others). **[VERIFY no 2025–26 bill changed this.]**
- **Marketing/solicitation (tightened):** **SB 90** (eff. Jan 1, 2024) requires a
  capitalized "THIS IS A SOLICITATION…" legend on unsolicited written offers;
  **HB 240** (eff. May 2, 2024) adds a fair-market-value caveat if a price is
  named. ~**$600/violation**. **[VERIFY code sections/penalties.]**
- **Pre-foreclosure:** no dedicated equity-purchaser statute; policed under the
  **Fair Business Practices Act (§10-1-390 et seq.)**. **[VERIFY gap.]**
- Sources: law.justia.com Title 43 Ch.40; rules.sos.ga.gov 520-1; law.georgia.gov

## OHIO
- **License + major new law:** **O.R.C. §5301.95** (SB 155, 136th G.A.) — effective
  **~March 2, 2026** **[VERIFY in force on your date]**. For 1–4 unit residential:
  a **standalone, bold ≥12-pt, signed** written disclosure to the record owner
  before any binding contract (you're not the end buyer, intend to assign, may
  profit, price may be below market, seller may seek counsel). Missing disclosure
  → owner may **cancel before close of escrow**, earnest money returned in 30 days.
- **Marketing:** market the equitable interest, not the property (Ch. 4735).
- **Pre-foreclosure:** no single named statute; hooks are Home Solicitation Sales
  Act (§1345.21, 3-day cancel) + CSPA (Ch. 1345). **[VERIFY.]**
- Sources: codes.ohio.gov §5301.95, §1345.21; legiscan.com OH SB155; com.ohio.gov

## NORTH CAROLINA
- **License:** No license to assign your own bona fide contract; brokerage for
  others needs a license (§93A-1/§93A-2). **NCREC is explicit/aggressive:** no
  "investor/cash-buyer" exemption; you may **not** negotiate between seller and
  buyer, do general buyer solicitation, or handle others' funds.
- **Pre-foreclosure (robust):** **Ch. 75 Art. 5A (§§75-120–122)** "Home
  Foreclosure Rescue Scams" — sale-leaseback/repurchase of a principal residence
  in default is unlawful for gain **unless transferee pays ≥50% of FMV**; remedy
  includes **voiding** the transaction.
- Sources: ncrec.gov (License Law + unlicensed-activity bulletin);
  ncleg.gov Ch.75 Art.5A

## Cross-cutting — TCPA / Do-Not-Call (all states)
- **Texts count.** DNC registry + prior-express-written-consent rules apply to
  calls/texts to cell numbers; autodialer/prerecorded restrictions apply.
- **One-to-one consent rule vacated** (11th Cir., Jan 2025) **[VERIFY posture]**.
- **Revocation rule (eff. Apr 11, 2025):** consumers may revoke "in any reasonable
  manner"; honor within 10 business days.
- **State mini-TCPAs:** FL FTSA (§501.059) notably stricter; check TX/GA/OH/NC.

## What to ask your attorney (per state)
- **TX:** exact §5.0205 date/text; is my dual written disclosure compliant; does a
  default-homeowner deal trigger Ch. 21 or executory-contract rules?
- **FL:** does my marketing stay in the §475.011 principal lane (avoid §475.42
  felony); §501.1377 compliance; FTSA exposure on calls/texts?
- **GA:** still no licensing requirement; do my mailers carry SB 90/HB 240 legends
  correctly; how does the FBPA apply to default homeowners?
- **OH:** is §5301.95 in force on my date; does my standalone bold disclosure meet
  it; which CSPA provisions govern default-homeowner buys?
- **NC:** do I market only the contract and avoid every NCREC unlicensed-conduct
  item; does any sale-leaseback comply with §§75-120–122 (≥50% FMV)?
- **All:** current TCPA/DNC posture (scrubbing, consent records, Apr-2025
  revocation rule, post-vacatur one-to-one status, state mini-TCPAs).
